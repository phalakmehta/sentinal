What the data taught us
  • Extremes are normal. 47 of 58 signals have heavy tails, so “big” does not mean “wrong”.
  • Rhythm matters. 18 signals repeat every day, so low at 3 a.m. can be fine.
  • No common scale. Percent, dollars, tweet counts, car speeds.
Preprocessing

Think of a security guard watching one number at a time. Five watchers look for five kinds of weird: a spike, a broken daily routine, a new level that stays, jumpy or frozen behaviour, and a shape never seen before. Each feeds a rarity meter: how often has this signal done something this extreme? An alarm needs something rarer than 1 in 100,000. After an alarm, it waits 300 values, so one incident gives one alarm. Every value gets a score, a label and, if flagged, a reason.
05The key idea · adaptive threshold

Don't alarm on a number. Alarm on rarity.

Each watcher keeps a rarity meter for its own signal. One rule everywhere, rarer than 1 in 100,000, so the same detector works on CPU, tweets and taxi rides with no threshold set by hand. (The maths: extreme-value theory.)

The threshold is a probability, not a number. Each watcher asks how often it has seen something this extreme on this particular signal; the alarm needs rarer than 1 in 100,000. The maths is extreme-value theory. Fix 1: with only a few extremes seen, the meter guessed huge values were normal; a 111-unit spike needed 690 to alarm. Now it needs 79, so it is caught. Biggest-ever spikes caught went from 31 to 37. Fix 2: if the alarm is still on after the waiting period, the signal has a new normal, so Sentinel re-learns it: 7 false alarms down to 1 on this disk signal.

10Honest limits · what's next

What it can't do yet, and what comes next.

caughtfalse alarmreal anomaly

Most of our 39 false alarms are big bursts like these that nobody labelled.

Known limits
  • “Seen before” spikes: when an equally big spike came earlier, rarity can't tell them apart. 11 of 32 caught.
  • Short hourly signals: little history to learn from. Online ads: 5 of 14.
  • Warm-up: the first 300 values are for learning only.
Next
  • Learn from feedback: “not a problem” stops that pattern alarming.
  • Weekly and holiday rhythm for demand signals.
  • Watch related signals together: CPU, disk and network of one machine.
